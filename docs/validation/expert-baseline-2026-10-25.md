# Expert baseline pilot, 2026-10-25

The replacement S0 measurement is complete as a reviewed, bounded local pilot. It did not establish a practical memory benefit, human-calibrated semantic acceptance or improved expertise. All four arms usually reached the relevant source material, but the reviewers gave practical decision usefulness only 1.75-1.83 out of 4 on average. Persistent notes did not fix missing qualifications, weak attribution or requested-form failures. The result supports retaining the measurement and its concrete repair evidence, not widening autonomy or making preparation default-on.

## What was measured

Twelve fictional decision cases cover three cumulative source worlds, four cases per world, and four paired arms: independent fresh study, raw current source history, a frozen initial brief and chronologically maintained notes. All answers receive the complete current-world source packet and common answer instructions; the declared bounded optional notes differ. Thus compiled-initial answers can correct old notes by reading new sources. This is not a test of recalling an update without seeing its source. Forty-eight answer cells are twelve paired cases, not 48 independent observations or real deployments.

The protocol, source packets, questions, source-grounded rubric, calibration and scope amendment were frozen before held-out generation. No answer-based setting change, rerun, clipping or favorable resampling was used. Two independently calibrated model reviewers labelled all 48 answers in different blinded orders. Their original matrices remain separate. Sixteen answers received a third blinded review of boolean disagreements or flagged false support before analysis; no original ordinal score was overwritten. Reviewers had procedural context separation, not established independent model-family errors. Calibration agreements establish consistency with reviewed model references, not human gold.

- Benchmark transfer: both reviewers found the compiled-initial answer usefully refused transferring a Linux single-producer median to a Windows SLA and recommended separate process spools.
