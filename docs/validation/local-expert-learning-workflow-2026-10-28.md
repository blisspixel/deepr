# Local expert learning workflow, 2026-10-28

## Result

The supervised workflow executed through formation, consultation, a local
experiment, outcome recording, retained evidence, study, perspective revision,
and later consultation. The semantic learning check failed: after reading and
retaining the experiment result, the revised expert denied that the experiment
had been executed. Persistence worked; dependable learning is not established.

This is an operational demonstration on source commit `f453a8d2`, not S1
qualification, S4 acceptance, a calibrated benchmark, or authority to widen
autonomy. No production expert was changed. No paid provider call was made.

## Case and prospective criteria

The synthetic Python fixture has two workers sharing a reservation counter but
keeping separate in-memory operation-key caches. A same-worker smoke test
passes. The initial source says a failure-injection experiment has not yet run.
The expert must assess a proposed timeout-retry policy and select a useful test.

The protocol and transfer question were saved before generation. Criteria were:
retain a source and form a perspective; propose a discriminating experiment;
execute and retain its result; explicitly revise the expert while preserving
history; use that evidence in a new-process consultation; and apply the lesson
to a separate label-printing case with a 60-second key cache and a possible
90-second queue delay. The transfer question supplies no printer transaction
or deduplication guarantee.

The experiment script was prepared before the first consultation. Its
lost-acknowledgement/cross-worker scenario matched the model's proposed test;
the supervising harness executed it. The model did not independently write or
execute the script. These are actual executions of a deterministic simulation,
not production measurements or independent validation of a real service.

## Semantic findings

Review was unblinded and source-grounded, without human calibration or an
independent scored reviewer panel. The observations below are diagnostic
findings, not an expertise grade.

1. The initial consultation selected the appropriate lost-acknowledgement and
   cross-worker experiment. Its remedy advice overreached: sticky routing,
   a shared cache, or two-phase commit were offered without adequately stating
   the additional persistence, atomicity and failure assumptions.
2. The new study combined the observed two-reservation result with the earlier
   statement that no failure-injection test had yet run. The revised brief
   continued to describe the exact experiment results as unresolved and
   cross-worker behavior as unvalidated.
3. Revised consultation `consult_f843d37f3785` said **no failure-injection
   experiment has yet been executed**. That is false for the scoped simulation
   that had just run. It is true that no real production/network experiment ran;
   the answer failed to preserve that distinction.
4. The exact delivered prompt contains both retained source identities and all
   three new findings. The general reference excerpt for the result starts
   mid-line and omits its title, observation time and the start of a case label.
   Stale study and brief statements are also delivered. This is not simply an
   absent-file failure, and the run does not isolate one causal component.
5. Supplying the complete result directly in a labeled diagnostic follow-up
   recovered the two-reservation observation, but the answer invented a
   concurrency failure for the sequential shared-registry control and attached
   an unsupported `Same-vendor verified` label. This diagnostic changes both
   evidence presentation and instructions; it is not a repaired held-out pass
   or proof that excerpt selection alone caused the failure.
6. The maintained expert's transfer answer began by denying that the lesson
   transfers, then described mechanisms that do transfer. It asserted that the
   printer lacks transaction/deduplication support, although that capability
   was unspecified, and assumed cache timing not supplied by the case.
7. An empty-profile CLI baseline was refused before generation with
   `skipped_no_valid_perspectives`. That refusal is retained and not scored as
   an answer. A separate direct native baseline then received both complete
   sources, the same transfer question, model, system instruction, temperature,
   context and generation ceiling. It also made material errors, including
   carrying the reservation counter into the printer test and dismissing the
   TTL concern too broadly. Different context construction and unequal total
   preprocessing mean this is descriptive, not a controlled benefit estimate.

The operational loop is demonstrated. The requirement that new observations
produce a sound durable revision and better subsequent guidance is not met.
The next repair target is reconciliation across initial evidence, later
observations, study findings, perspective and delivered advice, with controls
against invented verification and overstated remedies. S2-S4 remain necessary;
no expansion of autonomous execution follows from this run.
