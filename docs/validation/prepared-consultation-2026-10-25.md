**Reviewer A:** the stored-context answer usefully rejected blanket safety and
proposed contention/termination tests, but attributed package existence to an

that 4.2.4 preceded Python 3.12 had no supplied historical support; its pressure

worded more broadly than a metadata declaration, omission claims conflicted

did not clearly limit its object to the matching downloaded wheel archive.
Both answers left bounded currentness underanswered and offered overly binary
acceptance conditions around clean advisory results and testing. This was not
an accepted prepared answer or causal preparation benefit.

**Reviewer B:** independently found helpful safety dissent in both answers and
the same unsupported chronology/age-based upgrade narrative in the stored
answer. B judged the prepared answer helpful metadata-informed caution with
material qualification, attribution and artifact-verification defects. Its
review stressed that `py3-none-any` and Requires-Python describe packaging intent
and declared eligibility, not verified wheel contents or correct Windows

it does not hash an installed directory, a source archive or metadata sidecar.
B also retained incomplete currentness, overly broad omission claims and

correct or evidence of improved judgment.

The reviewers agree on the main repair needs, while their complete rationales
remain separate rather than being averaged or replaced by a consensus verdict:

- Distinguish unknown safety from evidence of an unsafe or outdated dependency.
  The stored answer's invented release age cannot justify an upgrade.
- State the positive bounded observation: 4.2.4 was the highest version listed

  not yanked at the October 26 UTC observation. This is neither a complete
  inventory guarantee nor future currentness or comprehensive safety.

  each digest prescription to the exact distribution artifact it identifies.
- Distinguish fields omitted from a projection, fields actually delivered in
  a raw excerpt, and URL targets/artifact contents never fetched. Both answers
  lacked precise source-ID/field attribution for their material conclusions.
- Keep old-version yanks scoped to those versions. Neither reviewer treated
  historical 3.15.0/3.15.2 yanks as evidence against the pinned 4.2.4 release.
